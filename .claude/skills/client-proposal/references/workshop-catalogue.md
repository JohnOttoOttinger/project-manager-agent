- **Sketching your grid & visuals** — before touching any tool

### B. Charts & Graphs

- **Chart types for reports** — charting for infographics and print *(Infographics)*
- **Removing chartjunk** — undoing software defaults *(PowerPoint)*

### C. Diagramming & visual vocabulary

> ⚠️ The dedicated **Storytelling with Data** workshop file is empty. The teaching

### H. AI in the data workflow — **exists as a live demo, not as a written module**

Verified 2 Sep 2026 by extracting 980 slides, 147 speaker-note sets and 1,013
media files across five client decks. Corrects an earlier note in this file that
said AI content did not exist.

**What is actually there — a reusable ~6-slide live segment:**

| Slide | Content |
|---|---|
| "One volunteer? + one A.I." | A live head-to-head — a participant races an AI on the workshop dataset |
| *(image slides)* | Screenshots of the outputs |
| The prompt slide | "Give me the top 5 insights from this dataset." · "Summarize this data and explain it to me like I'm an 8th grader." · "Tell me two calls-to-action to tell my stakeholders about this data." |
| The dashboard prompt | "Visualize this data in a five-module dashboard." |

**It travels.** The same prompt slide appears verbatim in
`ZIM/Datalabs-ZIM-Visual-Storytelling-Attempt-v2.pptx` (slide 299) and
`IEEFA/DatalabsAgency-Energy-Workshop-IEEFA-v3.pptx` (slide 382). IEEFA also
carries a worked "insight statement" example (slide 204 — Indonesian coal and
renewables). IEEFA v3 is titled *"Data Visualization, AI, & Storytelling"*, so AI
has already been sold as a named component of a paid workshop.

**Why it is not written down:** Otto develops this content live, in the room, and
extends it workshop to workshop. That is the reason it is strong and the reason
it resists documentation.

**How to sell it without over-promising.** Describe the *mechanism*, not a
curriculum: "a live head-to-head — one of your people against an AI, on your own
dataset, with the prompts on screen." That is honest, it is differentiated,
nobody can copy an improvised demo, and it commits to no learning outcome that an
improvised segment cannot deliver. Do **not** write formal AI learning outcomes
into a proposal until the module is actually written.

**Search tip that mattered:** searching these decks for "AI" finds almost nothing.
Search for **prompt language** instead — "give me the top", "summarise this",
"explain it to me like", "visualise this". That is how the content is written.

## Assembling an agenda from client interests

Map what the client named to modules, then check coverage before quoting.

| Client says | Modules | Coverage |
|---|---|---|
| "Charts and graphs" | B | Strong |
| "Diagramming" | C | **Thin** |
| "Storytelling with data" | D | Strong, but scattered |
| "The different formats" | E | Strong — lead with the bridging module |
| "Dashboards" | F | Strong, with assets |
| "PowerPoint" | B, D, E, G | Strong |
| "Our own data / our sector" | any | Depends on prior client work — check `Client Projects/` |
| "AI" | H | Live demo segment, proven in 2 client workshops; no written outcomes |

**Always check `~/Documents/Datalabs - iCloud Apple/Client Projects/<Client>/` and
`Old Datalabs - Refile & Delete/Clients/<Client>/` before writing an agenda.**
Prior work for the same account means the exercises can run on data they already
recognise, which is the strongest form of tailoring available and is routinely
forgotten.
