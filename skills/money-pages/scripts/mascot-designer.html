Questions about

Mascot design?

[/vc_column_text][dfd_spacer screen_wide_resolution="1280" screen_wide_spacer_size="40" screen_normal_resolution="1024" screen_tablet_resolution="800" screen_mobile_resolution="480" screen_normal_spacer_size="30" screen_tablet_spacer_size="20" screen_mobile_spacer_size="20"][dfd_accordion style="style-3" active_section="1" font_size="18" tab_title_google_fonts="yes" tab_title_custom_fonts="font_family:Arvo%3Aregular%2Citalic%2C700%2C700italic|font_style:700%20bold%20regular%3A700%3Anormal" icon_size="14"][vc_tta_section title="What does a mascot designer actually do?" tab_id="1757200000001-mascot-designer-faq-1"][vc_column_text css=""]

The job splits in two: who the character is, and what the costume has to survive. That means the face and the personality, and it also means the performer inside it - sightlines, ventilation, weight, how long a shift can run, and whether the costume fits through a standard doorway.

[/vc_column_text][/vc_tta_section][vc_tta_section title="Do you manufacture the costume?" tab_id="1757200000002-mascot-designer-faq-2"][vc_column_text css=""]

No. Oddtoe designs; a specialist costume manufacturer builds. The design package is made to be handed straight to a maker - 3D model, turnarounds, colour and material specification - so the build starts from a signed-off character rather than an interpretation of a drawing.

[/vc_column_text][/vc_tta_section][vc_tta_section title="How long does it take to get a mascot made?" tab_id="1757200000003-mascot-designer-faq-3"][vc_column_text css=""]

Manufacture typically runs eight to twelve weeks, excluding delivery, which is the standard lead time across specialist costume makers. Design sits in front of that and takes two to three weeks. Commission today and the first appearance is realistically three to four months away.

[/vc_column_text][/vc_tta_section][vc_tta_section title="Do I need to supply a drawing?" tab_id="1757200000004-mascot-designer-faq-4"][vc_column_text css=""]

No. Most briefs arrive as a description, a logo, or a rough sketch, and some arrive with nothing at all. The first stage is working out what the character is and what it has to do, which happens before anything is drawn.

[/vc_column_text][/vc_tta_section][vc_tta_section title="How much does a mascot cost?" tab_id="1757200000005-mascot-designer-faq-5"][vc_column_text css=""]

Cost is driven by what the brief decides: how structural the character is, how many costumes are needed, whether it requires cooling, and whether the design also has to work as an inflatable or on screen. Send the brief and where it will be used, and you get a quote.

[/vc_column_text][/vc_tta_section][vc_tta_section title="What is the difference between a mascot and a brand character?" tab_id="1757200000006-mascot-designer-faq-6"][vc_column_text css=""]

A brand character lives in artwork - packaging, advertising, animation. Build it so a person can wear it and stand in a crowd and you have a mascot. Many brand characters need redesigning to make that move, because a design that reads flat does not always read at three metres with a human inside it.

[/vc_column_text][/vc_tta_section][vc_tta_section title="Can the same character work as a giant inflatable?" tab_id="1757200000007-mascot-designer-faq-7"][vc_column_text css=""]

Yes, and it is worth deciding early. A character designed once in 3D can be built as a worn costume, as a giant inflatable, and as animated content for screens or projection. Retro-fitting a flat mascot drawing into a ten-metre inflatable afterwards usually means redesigning it.

[/vc_column_text][/vc_tta_section][vc_tta_section title="Where is Oddtoe based?" tab_id="1757200000008-mascot-designer-faq-8"][vc_column_text css=""]

Oddtoe is based in Melbourne, Australia. Supplier and business contacts run through Berlin and Los Angeles, and those are the starting points for work in Europe and the United States.
