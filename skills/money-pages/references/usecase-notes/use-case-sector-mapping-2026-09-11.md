# Use Cases: what each audience would actually use Oddtoe for

For Otto, 11 Sep 2026. Second version. Each section starts from the buyer's year, lists the jobs
they would hire Oddtoe for, and then picks the one that becomes the use case page. The decision
is at the end.

---

## 1. A museum or cultural institution

**Their year.** A programming team runs a calendar: two or three big exhibitions, school-holiday
programs, an adults-only late opening once a month or once a season, a members' night, a sleepover
for kids, a fundraising gala, and one anniversary or festival tie-in. Marketing has one job across
all of it: get people through the door who do not usually come, and get a photograph that proves
it happened.

**What they would use Oddtoe for:**
- **Something to fill the building after dark.** A late opening needs one thing people photograph.
  Projection on the facade or in the atrium, an illuminated inflatable in the forecourt, a kinetic
  piece in the foyer.
- **Something for children to sleep under.** The sleepover is the same brief at a smaller scale: a
  walk-in object in the great hall that is the memory of the night.
- **A character for the kids' program.** A mascot or drawn character for school holidays, on the
  signage, the map, the activity sheet, and in costume on the day.
- **Interpretation for an exhibition.** Short animated pieces that explain an object or a period,
  drawn in the museum's voice rather than stock footage.
- **A forecourt piece for the season.** Topiary, a sculpture, or an inflatable that stands for the
  run of the exhibition and comes down with it.
- **A centrepiece for the gala.** One object on the night that the donors stand next to.

**The use case page: Museum Late Opening.** It is the brief with a date, a marketing owner, a
budget line, and a photograph as the deliverable. The other five become the six ideas on that
page (the sleepover, the kids' character, the forecourt piece, the exhibition animation, the gala
object, the facade projection). "Who commissions" names programming, public engagement, marketing,
and the friends' organisation.

## 2. An events manager

**Their year.** A festival producer or an events agency has a fixed set of dates and a venue for
each: a city festival, a corporate conference or gala, a brand activation for a client, a council
event, a Christmas takeover. They hire stages, lighting, AV, caterers and crews. The recurring
problem is that every event looks like the last one, and the client asks what will be different.

**What they would use Oddtoe for:**
- **The entrance.** A walk-in inflatable or a built gateway that says the event has started.
- **The photo spot.** One object on site that people queue to stand in front of.
- **The venue after dark.** Projection on the building or in the hall for the evening session.
- **A parade or procession piece.** Carried, pulled or worn, for a street festival.
- **A seasonal takeover.** A Christmas or summer installation for a precinct or a hospitality
  group's venues, up for six weeks.
- **A mascot for the festival.** A character that comes back every year.

**The use case page: Festival or Precinct Activation.** The brief is a date and a space that needs
one thing people remember. The six ideas are the list above. Hospitality lives here: venue
openings, rooftop bars, seasonal takeovers are events with a venue, not a separate audience.

## 3. Real estate and architecture

**Their year.** A developer has a launch for each project and a sales suite that has to sell
off-the-plan for a year. An architect or interior designer has a lobby, an atrium or a public
space to finish, sometimes with a council condition to spend a percentage on public art. Both
ask on behalf of a client and need something they can present as a picture before it exists.

**What they would use Oddtoe for:**
- **The lobby piece.** A sculpture, a kinetic work or a topiary form that gives the building its
  photograph and its name in the listing.
- **The hoarding.** A mural or a drawn story on the construction fence for the eighteen months
  before anything else is visible.
- **The launch night.** Projection on the site or the display suite, one evening, for the press
  and the first buyers.
- **The public-art condition.** A commissioned piece that satisfies the council and that the
  architect is happy to stand next to.
- **The garden.** A sensory garden or topiary for the podium or the courtyard.
- **The fly-through.** An animated walk through the building for the sales suite, drawn rather
  than rendered, so it looks like a place and not a render.

**The use case page: Lobby or Development Launch.** The brief is a building with a date and a
client who has to approve a picture first. The timeline ends on handover and maintenance rather
than a press pickup, which is the only structural difference from the other pages.

## 4. An entertainment producer or publisher

**Their year.** A producer develops slates: pitches to broadcasters and streamers, looks for
formats and characters they can own, needs a pilot or a short to prove an idea. A publisher looks
for series, strips and characters with an audience. Both buy ideas with an author attached, and
both need material to show a commissioner: a bible, a character sheet, an animatic, a pilot.

**What they would use Oddtoe for:**
- **An original series.** Satire or comedy with a point of view, pitched with characters and a
  pilot script, like The Oddtoe TV Show or Wire Taps.
- **A short to test an idea.** Generative-AI animation gets a pilot made at a cost a producer can
  risk.
- **Documentary animation.** Sequences for a documentary where there is no footage.
- **A strip or gag series.** A daily or weekly panel for a publisher, drawn by a former political
  cartoonist.
- **A character the brand can own.** Character design for a franchise, a channel or a game.
- **A stunt filmed as content.** The publicity stunt page's object, shot as an episode.

**The use case page: New Content.** This is the one page where the object is a story, not a
thing. The timeline runs premise, characters, script, animatic, pilot, distribution. The spec strip
counts episodes and minutes, not crates. Everything else on the template holds.

## 5. An art director or marketing executive

**Their year.** A brand marketer runs launches, campaigns and content. Each quarter has a product
or a campaign that needs a picture, a social moment, and something for retail. The agency art
director is asked for the idea that is not another billboard. This is where the competitor's
sector list lives: drinks, food, beauty, fashion, tech are all this buyer with a different product
in their hand.

**What they would use Oddtoe for:**
- **The giant product.** A three-metre bottle, jar, lipstick or headset on a plinth for launch
  day, then touring retailers.
- **A brand character.** Designed once, used on pack, in animation, in costume.
- **The stunt.** The publicity stunt page, for a brand rather than a campaign.
- **The mural.** Street art for a launch, on a wall the audience already walks past.
- **Animated content.** Short pieces for social built on the same character or object.
- **The pop-up.** A built object that makes a pop-up shop or a press day worth photographing.

**The use case page: Product Launch.** It is the brief every one of those sectors budgets for, and
the giant product is the object prop fabricators sell most, without the design step. The six ideas
are one per sector. "Who commissions" names brand managers, product marketing and their agencies.
