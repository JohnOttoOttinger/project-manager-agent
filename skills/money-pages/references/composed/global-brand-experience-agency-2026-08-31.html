What does a global brand experience agency actually do?

[/dfd_heading][dfd_spacer screen_wide_resolution="1280" screen_wide_spacer_size="20" screen_normal_resolution="1024" screen_tablet_resolution="800" screen_mobile_resolution="480" screen_normal_spacer_size="20" screen_tablet_spacer_size="20" screen_mobile_spacer_size="20"][vc_column_text css=""]

A global brand experience agency designs one concept and the system for repeating it well, rather than briefing a new idea into every city on the schedule. Oddtoe designs the piece, writes the build spec it travels on, and works with vetted local partners so each market's build matches that spec precisely.

Oddtoe is an experiential design and generative-AI animation studio based in Melbourne, creating projection, installation, and animated work for events, venues, and galleries.

Local vendor or global brand experience agency, and does the difference matter?

[/dfd_heading][dfd_spacer screen_wide_resolution="1280" screen_wide_spacer_size="20" screen_normal_resolution="1024" screen_tablet_resolution="800" screen_mobile_resolution="480" screen_normal_spacer_size="20" screen_tablet_spacer_size="20" screen_mobile_spacer_size="20"][vc_column_text css=""]

A local vendor builds what you already designed, in one city. A global brand experience agency designs the concept and the spec for repeating it well, so the version in Singapore and the version in Sydney both read as the same brand, run by people who know the market they are actually building in.

The distinction matters most once a second market joins the schedule. Briefing each city separately means the idea gets reinterpreted every time, and a small drift in one build reads as a different brand by the third city. An agency that owns the concept keeps that drift out by design, not by luck.

It matters again on who finds the local hands. Sourcing and vetting a production partner in a city you do not work in is its own project, done on top of the actual creative work. Oddtoe runs that search, hands the local partner a build-ready spec instead of a moodboard, and stays the single point of contact while it happens.

It cuts both ways: for one activation in one city you already know, a local specialist is often the simpler buy. See installation artist or projection artist for what the studio builds directly.

It depends on how many markets the rollout covers, how much original design the concept needs, and how much of the local build the agency runs versus hands off. A single-city activation and a five-market rollout of the same idea sit at very different ends. Oddtoe quotes per project after a scoping conversation, so the number reflects your brief rather than a rate card.

Check whether they design the concept once and adapt it, or redesign it per market, whether one team owns the whole rollout, and how they find and manage local production partners. Ask to see the build spec behind a past multi-market job — the working document a local partner actually received.

What can a global brand experience agency deliver across markets?

[/dfd_heading][dfd_spacer screen_wide_resolution="1280" screen_wide_spacer_size="20" screen_normal_resolution="1024" screen_tablet_resolution="800" screen_mobile_resolution="480" screen_normal_spacer_size="20" screen_tablet_spacer_size="20" screen_mobile_spacer_size="20"][vc_column_text css=""]

Projection and installation pieces, generative-AI animated content, and the production documentation that lets a local partner build the same piece correctly in a different city. Oddtoe designs the concept once and hands local partners a build-ready spec they can follow directly.

Each of those is a craft in its own right. Installation covers the built environments and structures. Projection takes the piece off the wall and onto a surface that already exists. Prop and 3D making covers the physical and digital objects a rollout needs, wherever it is fabricated.

Where a brief calls for it the studio also works in generative AI, as one technique among several rather than the whole pitch. There is a fuller account on the generative AI animator page.

How do you choose a global brand experience agency?

[/dfd_heading][dfd_spacer screen_wide_resolution="1280" screen_wide_spacer_size="20" screen_normal_resolution="1024" screen_tablet_resolution="800" screen_mobile_resolution="480" screen_normal_spacer_size="20" screen_tablet_spacer_size="20" screen_mobile_spacer_size="20"][vc_column_text css=""]

Three tests. Do they design the concept once and adapt it, instead of reinventing it per market? Will one team own the whole rollout, start to finish? And how do they find and manage the local partners who actually build it? Ask for a build spec from a past job, and who on the ground actually built it.

The concept test catches the most common failure mode. An agency that treats every market as a fresh brief will show you five different-looking activations and call it localisation. It is really just drift.

The ownership test matters because a multi-market rollout runs long and touches more people than a single build. If the person who understood the original brief is not the person managing the third city, you will explain it all again, in a language the local build crew does not share.

The local-partner test is the one clients skip because it feels like a logistics question, not a creative one. It is both. I would rather show you exactly who builds it on the ground and how the spec gets handed over, before you sign anything.

[/vc_column_text][dfd_spacer screen_wide_resolution="1280" screen_wide_spacer_size="20" screen_normal_resolution="1024" screen_tablet_resolution="800" screen_mobile_resolution="480" screen_normal_spacer_size="20" screen_tablet_spacer_size="20" screen_mobile_spacer_size="20"][/vc_column_inner][vc_column_inner width="1/4"][/vc_column_inner][/vc_row_inner][/vc_column][vc_column][dfd_spacer screen_wide_resolution="1280" screen_wide_spacer_size="40" screen_normal_resolution="1024" screen_tablet_resolution="800" screen_mobile_resolution="480" screen_normal_spacer_size="40" screen_tablet_spacer_size="40" screen_mobile_spacer_size="40"][/vc_column][/vc_row][vc_row][vc_column][vc_column_text]

One concept, run correctly in more than one market

[/dfd_heading][dfd_spacer screen_wide_resolution="1280" screen_wide_spacer_size="20" screen_normal_resolution="1024" screen_tablet_resolution="800" screen_mobile_resolution="480" screen_normal_spacer_size="20" screen_tablet_spacer_size="15" screen_mobile_spacer_size="19"][vc_column_text css=""]

The pitch for briefing each market separately is that it is simpler, and for a single activation it often is. The cost that does not show up in any one quote is consistency. Somebody has to hold the original idea in their head while three different local vendors interpret it three different ways, and decide which version is actually right when they disagree. On a direct-hire rollout that somebody is you.

That is the part a global brand experience agency absorbs. I take a brief, design the concept once, and write the build spec that a local partner in any city can follow without guessing what I meant. You get one creative direction and one point of contact, and the version that lands in each market stays a build of the same idea, executed to the same spec.

The second thing an agency buys is the local search. Finding a fabricator or installer you trust, in a city you have never worked in, on a deadline, is its own project. I run that search, brief the partner against the spec, and stay on the job while they build, so you are not left managing a stranger on the other side of the world by yourself.

None of that makes an agency the right answer every time. A single activation in a city the client already knows does not need a travelling concept or a spec written for handoff. The rule I use is simple: the more markets a brief touches, and the more it needs to look like one brand doing it, the more that coordination is worth paying for.

Most of the work I get asked about starts as a single-market conversation that grows once the client sees it working. That is a reasonable way to test an idea before committing a whole rollout to it, and it is why the installation and projection pages exist as starting points in their own right.

How to brief a global brand experience agency

[/dfd_heading][dfd_spacer screen_wide_resolution="1280" screen_wide_spacer_size="20" screen_normal_resolution="1024" screen_tablet_resolution="800" screen_mobile_resolution="480" screen_normal_spacer_size="20" screen_tablet_spacer_size="15" screen_mobile_spacer_size="19"][vc_column_text css=""]

Tell me the outcome you want, and let the shot list come later. Who it is for, which cities or markets it needs to run in, when each one has to be live, and one or two references for the feeling you want. Rough is fine. Working the plan out from a loose brief is part of what you are paying for.

Four things change a quote more than anything else: how many markets it runs in, the technique, how much original design the concept needs, and how much of the local build the agency runs versus hands to your own team on the ground.

Say what you already have. Brand guidelines, a global campaign brief, existing local vendor relationships, even a rollout that went wrong last time all shorten the scoping conversation. So does saying which market has to launch first.

If you are still deciding what the activation even looks like, say that too. The studio keeps a guide to activation formats worth a look before the brief is locked. Send it through the contact page and a real person reads it.
