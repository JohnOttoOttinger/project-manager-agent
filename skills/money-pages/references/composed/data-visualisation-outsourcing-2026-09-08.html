Outsourcing makes sense when you need dashboard or data-visualisation work at a level of polish your team cannot deliver alone, without carrying a full-time salary for it. Keeping it in-house makes sense when the work is constant enough to fill a real role. Most teams sit somewhere in between, which is why the comparison below exists.

When does an in-house hire make more sense?

[/dfd_heading][dfd_spacer screen_wide_resolution="1280" screen_wide_spacer_size="20" screen_normal_resolution="1024" screen_tablet_resolution="800" screen_mobile_resolution="480" screen_normal_spacer_size="20" screen_tablet_spacer_size="20" screen_mobile_spacer_size="20"][vc_column_text css=""]

If dashboard or reporting work lands on your desk every week rather than every quarter, a full-time hire usually costs less over a year than repeated outside engagements, and builds context about your business that no outside partner can shortcut.

A specialist agency is not the right fit for constant, day-to-day reporting work inside one team — that is exactly when an in-house hire pays for itself. Where an outside partner still adds value even then is the parts that benefit from a fresh eye once: a style guide, an initial workshop to set the design standards, or a dashboard rebuild your own team can maintain afterward.

What do you actually give up when you outsource this work?

[/dfd_heading][dfd_spacer screen_wide_resolution="1280" screen_wide_spacer_size="20" screen_normal_resolution="1024" screen_tablet_resolution="800" screen_mobile_resolution="480" screen_normal_spacer_size="20" screen_tablet_spacer_size="15" screen_mobile_spacer_size="19"][vc_column_text css=""]

I get asked some version of this question every month: should a company hire someone, hire a freelancer, or bring in The Datalabs Agency? Outsourcing is not free of trade-offs just because it is common. You give up some day-to-day control, and you give up the accumulated context an in-house person builds after a year on your team.

In exchange, you get a team that has already made most of its mistakes on someone else’s project. I founded The Datalabs Agency in 2012, and the design process behind every dashboard, style guide, and workshop we deliver has been tested on clients including Mercedes-Benz, Adidas, and UPS. A freelancer starting a new engagement, or a new in-house hire in their first quarter, has not had that many chances to fail safely yet.

The trade-off changes shape depending on what you are outsourcing. Handing over one dashboard is low-risk either way — worst case, you pay again for a second attempt. Handing over your team’s entire reporting standard is different: a BI style guide set by an outside partner has to survive years of use by people who were not in the room when it was designed. That is where our data visualisation style guides work spends most of its effort — a system built to hold up years after the handover meeting, not just impress in it.

None of this means outsourcing is always the answer. If I think a client would be better served by hiring someone permanent, I say so — the FAQ above spells out when an in-house hire wins. What I will not do is sell a one-off engagement as a substitute for a role that clearly needs to exist inside a company, because that model breaks down as soon as the next dashboard change comes through and nobody in the building knows why the last one looks the way it does.

How I decide what to recommend, even when it isn't us

[/dfd_heading][dfd_spacer screen_wide_resolution="1280" screen_wide_spacer_size="20" screen_normal_resolution="1024" screen_tablet_resolution="800" screen_mobile_resolution="480" screen_normal_spacer_size="20" screen_tablet_spacer_size="15" screen_mobile_spacer_size="19"][vc_column_text css=""]

Every outsourcing conversation starts the same way for me: what does the client actually need built, and how often will they need it again? A dashboard that gets rebuilt once a year is a different conversation from one that changes every sprint. I ask this before I talk about price, because the answer decides which of the three paths in the comparison above actually fits.

When a freelancer is actually the better fit for a small one-off job, I say so — a single branded chart for one presentation does not need a twelve-year-old design process behind it.

When the answer is an in-house hire, I say that too. A company running dashboard requests through my inbox every single week would save money within a year by hiring someone permanent, and I would rather tell a prospective client that up front than keep invoicing consulting hours that no longer make sense for their situation.

What I actually get asked to do most often sits in the middle: a team that already has someone covering day-to-day reporting, who needs a specialist for one dashboard rebuild, one BI style guide, or one round of team training a year. See our workshop pricing page if that last one is the piece you are missing, and our case studies for what that has looked like for clients like Mercedes-Benz and Adidas.
