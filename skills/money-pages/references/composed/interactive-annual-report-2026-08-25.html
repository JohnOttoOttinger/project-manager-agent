What is an interactive annual report?

[/dfd_heading][dfd_spacer screen_wide_resolution="1280" screen_wide_spacer_size="20" screen_normal_resolution="1024" screen_tablet_resolution="800" screen_mobile_resolution="480" screen_normal_spacer_size="20" screen_tablet_spacer_size="20" screen_mobile_spacer_size="20"][vc_column_text css=""]

An interactive annual report is a scrollable web page or microsite that presents a company’s yearly results, highlights, and data instead of, or alongside, a printed or PDF report. Readers click and scroll through sections instead of downloading a file, and every chart, photo, and figure can be swapped out the following year without a new print run.

The Datalabs Agency is a Melbourne-based data visualization consultancy founded in 2012 that delivers corporate training workshops (Power BI, Tableau, data storytelling), dashboard design, and BI style guides for clients including Mercedes-Benz, Adidas, and UPS.

Do you need special software for an interactive annual report?

[/dfd_heading][dfd_spacer screen_wide_resolution="1280" screen_wide_spacer_size="20" screen_normal_resolution="1024" screen_tablet_resolution="800" screen_mobile_resolution="480" screen_normal_spacer_size="20" screen_tablet_spacer_size="20" screen_mobile_spacer_size="20"][vc_column_text css=""]

No. This lives as a normal page on your existing website. There is no separate annual-report software to buy, install, or renew a licence for — The Datalabs Agency designs and builds the page directly, so there is nothing new for your investor relations or communications team to learn.

Searches for “annual report software” usually mean one of two things: a flipbook tool that turns a PDF into a page-turning animation, or a dedicated microsite platform with its own subscription and login. Both add an ongoing licence fee and a separate system for your team to manage alongside your main website. Our approach at The Datalabs Agency is different: we design the report as pages that live on your own domain, built with the same data visualization and dashboard design skills we use for corporate clients. That means one website to maintain, one login your communications team already has, and no annual software renewal beyond your normal hosting costs.

Why I design annual reports as pages, not PDFs

[/dfd_heading][dfd_spacer screen_wide_resolution="1280" screen_wide_spacer_size="20" screen_normal_resolution="1024" screen_tablet_resolution="800" screen_mobile_resolution="480" screen_normal_spacer_size="20" screen_tablet_spacer_size="15" screen_mobile_spacer_size="19"][vc_row_inner][vc_column_inner width="1/2"][vc_column_text css=""]

I have spent more than a decade designing how organisations present their own data, and a PDF is one of the worst formats for it. A PDF is built to be printed, not read on a screen — it fights you on a phone, it cannot be updated once it is out, and every chart inside it is a flat image no one can explore further. An interactive annual report fixes all three problems by being what it actually is: a web page.

I started thinking about visual storytelling at the National Geographic Society, where the whole point was making a reader stop and look closer at something they would otherwise skim past. An annual report faces the same problem — most readers open it once, skim the summary, and never touch the detailed numbers. A scrollable, well-designed web version invites the kind of closer look a stapled PDF never gets.

The brief is usually the same: take a year’s worth of results, decide which numbers deserve a chart and which deserve a sentence, and build a structure a stakeholder can navigate in under a minute. That is dashboard design work wearing an annual-report hat — the same principles behind our dashboard design projects apply directly to laying out a year’s highlights.

[/vc_column_text][/vc_column_inner][vc_column_inner width="1/2"][vc_column_text css=""]

What changes most for clients is what happens after launch. A printed report is finished the day it goes to the printer; a web-based one keeps working. We can swap a stat, add a late-breaking result, or fix a typo without reprinting a single page or reissuing a file to a distribution list. That flexibility alone is usually what convinces a communications team to make the switch.

None of this replaces a compliance filing — if your organisation is required to lodge a PDF, keep doing that. What it replaces is the version most people actually read: the one your website links to, the one you email to stakeholders, the one that still looks current the day before next year’s report goes live.

What actually goes into an interactive annual report

[/dfd_heading][dfd_spacer screen_wide_resolution="1280" screen_wide_spacer_size="20" screen_normal_resolution="1024" screen_tablet_resolution="800" screen_mobile_resolution="480" screen_normal_spacer_size="20" screen_tablet_spacer_size="15" screen_mobile_spacer_size="19"][vc_row_inner][vc_column_inner width="1/2"][vc_column_text css=""]

Every interactive annual report I build starts the same way: an audit of what you already have. That means the current PDF or print report, the underlying data behind each chart, your brand guidelines, and any photography or illustration you already own. Most of that content is reusable — the job is restructuring it for a screen instead of a page.

From there I map the report into sections a reader can jump between: a highlights summary, a letter from leadership, the year’s results broken into a handful of charts, and whatever else your organisation reports on. Each section is built to stand on its own, because most readers will land on one section from a link or a search instead of starting at page one.

Charts get rebuilt as fully interactive visualisations instead of embedded images wherever the data supports it, which is where the same data visualization discipline behind our corporate dashboard design work carries over directly — choosing the chart type that shows the number honestly, not the one that looks most dramatic.

[/vc_column_text][/vc_column_inner][vc_column_inner width="1/2"][vc_column_text css=""]

Once the structure and charts are in place, the report gets built on your own site instead of a separate platform, so your team keeps one login, one hosting bill, and one place to make next year’s update. If you want to see what the finished format looks like, browse our digital annual reports and microsites examples before we start scoping your version.

The whole point of building it this way is that year two costs less work than year one. Once the structure exists, refreshing it each year is largely a content update: new numbers, a new highlights section, maybe a new chart — not a full rebuild from a blank page.
