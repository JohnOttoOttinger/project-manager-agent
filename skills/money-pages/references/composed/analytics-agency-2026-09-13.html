What does a digital analytics agency do?

A digital analytics agency turns raw numbers from tools like Google Analytics, Power BI and Tableau into dashboards, reports and presentations that non-technical stakeholders actually use. We focus on the layer between the data and the decision — design, storytelling and dashboard build — not tag installation.

How is this different to a Google Analytics agency?

[/dfd_heading][dfd_spacer screen_wide_resolution="1280" screen_wide_spacer_size="20" screen_normal_resolution="1024" screen_tablet_resolution="800" screen_mobile_resolution="480" screen_normal_spacer_size="20" screen_tablet_spacer_size="20" screen_mobile_spacer_size="20"][vc_column_text css=""]

A typical Google Analytics agency configures tracking, tags and reports inside GA4. We start after that step: once your team already has the numbers, we turn them into dashboards, BI style guides and executive reporting your leadership can actually read and act on.

A typical “Google Analytics agency” spends its time inside GA4: tracking plans, event configuration, consent settings, and monthly performance reports. That is valuable work, and it is not ours. We start once your team already has numbers somewhere — Google Analytics, Power BI, a CRM, a spreadsheet — and turn them into something people outside the data team will actually open. Whether you run analytics in-house or through a specialist Google Analytics agency, we plug in as the design and reporting layer between the raw numbers and the boardroom. If your team needs the earlier stage — data collected and analysed from the ground up — our data analytics and reporting service covers that; this page is about what happens once the numbers already exist.

Why we treat analytics as a design problem

[/dfd_heading][dfd_spacer screen_wide_resolution="1280" screen_wide_spacer_size="20" screen_normal_resolution="1024" screen_tablet_resolution="800" screen_mobile_resolution="480" screen_normal_spacer_size="20" screen_tablet_spacer_size="15" screen_mobile_spacer_size="19"][vc_column_text css=""]

Most companies do not have an analytics problem. They have a communication problem: the data is already sitting in Google Analytics, Power BI, or a CRM that nobody outside the analytics team opens. We have watched that pattern for more than a decade, across workshops and dashboard projects for Mercedes-Benz, Adidas, and UPS, and it rarely changes with more dashboards — it changes with better ones.

A dashboard full of charts is not automatically useful. Design decides whether a stakeholder reads it in ten seconds or ignores it for a week: which number leads, what gets a colour, what gets left off entirely. That is the layer we work in — one level up from the analytics platform itself, whichever one your team already uses.

We are tool-agnostic on purpose. The same design thinking works whether your team lives in Power BI, Tableau, Excel, or PowerPoint, because the hard part was never the software — it is deciding what the dashboard is actually for. A BI style guide exists to answer that once, so every dashboard your team builds afterwards follows the same rules automatically.

That approach is why our workshops cover data storytelling and infographic design alongside tool-specific sessions — see the full list on our training workshops page. A team that understands the design principles gets more out of every dashboard we hand over, long after the engagement ends.
