MULTIDIMENSIONAL CHARTS

[/dfd_heading][ultimate_spacer height="20" height_on_tabs="20" height_on_tabs_portrait="20" height_on_mob_landscape="20" height_on_mob="20"][/vc_column][/vc_row][vc_row gap="20" bg_check="row-background-dark" css=".vc_custom_1696657851210{background-color: #2f2e3a !important;}"][vc_column][dfd_heading module_animation="transition.fadeIn" style="style_01" title_font_options="tag:h2|font_size:96|line_height:100|color:%23ffffff" subtitle_font_options="tag:h3" heading_margin="margin-top:20px;"]Pie or Donut Chart[/dfd_heading][vc_column_text css=""]

Best for: one whole, few parts

family: charts & graphs

[/vc_column_text][ultimate_spacer height="20" height_on_tabs="20" height_on_tabs_portrait="20" height_on_mob_landscape="20" height_on_mob="20"][ultimate_spacer height="20" height_on_tabs="20" height_on_tabs_portrait="20" height_on_mob_landscape="20" height_on_mob="20"][/vc_column][vc_column width="1/6"][/vc_column][vc_column width="1/4"][dfd_single_image image="37170" image_size="custom" image_width="300" image_height="300" hover_style="dfd-image-scale" image_border_radius="10"][ultimate_spacer height="20" height_on_tabs="20" height_on_tabs_portrait="20" height_on_mob_landscape="20" height_on_mob="20"][/vc_column][vc_column width="5/12"][vc_column_text]

A pie chart splits a single whole into shares your audience can read in two seconds.

Slice count is the whole game. Keep it to five segments or fewer, lead with the biggest share, and the chart does its job before anyone reads a label. The donut variant frees the centre for a headline number, which is why it survives every design fashion cycle.

Once shares get close in size, human eyes stop judging angles well; a humble bar chart beats it there every time.

Best for: the shape of one variable

family: charts & graphs

[/vc_column_text][ultimate_spacer height="20" height_on_tabs="20" height_on_tabs_portrait="20" height_on_mob_landscape="20" height_on_mob="20"][ultimate_spacer height="20" height_on_tabs="20" height_on_tabs_portrait="20" height_on_mob_landscape="20" height_on_mob="20"][/vc_column][vc_column width="1/6"][/vc_column][vc_column width="5/12"][vc_column_text]

A histogram shows how one measure is distributed, from the runt of the litter to the outliers.

Salary bands, delivery times, response scores; whenever the question is “what is typical and what is extreme?”, this is the chart. Bars touch because the data is continuous, and the bin width you choose quietly decides the story.

Too few bins hides the pattern, too many turns it to noise, so we test several widths before settling.

Best for: two measures, hunting a relationship

family: charts & graphs

[/vc_column_text][ultimate_spacer height="20" height_on_tabs="20" height_on_tabs_portrait="20" height_on_mob_landscape="20" height_on_mob="20"][ultimate_spacer height="20" height_on_tabs="20" height_on_tabs_portrait="20" height_on_mob_landscape="20" height_on_mob="20"][/vc_column][vc_column width="1/6"][/vc_column][vc_column width="1/4"][dfd_single_image image="37098" image_size="custom" image_width="300" image_height="300" hover_style="dfd-image-scale" image_border_radius="10"][ultimate_spacer height="20" height_on_tabs="20" height_on_tabs_portrait="20" height_on_mob_landscape="20" height_on_mob="20"][/vc_column][vc_column width="5/12"][vc_column_text]

A scatter plot throws every record onto two axes and lets the pattern speak.

Marketing spend against revenue, training hours against error rates; the cloud of dots reveals correlation, clusters and outliers in one view. Colour or size can smuggle in a third variable when the story needs it.

Executives sometimes find raw scatters busy, so we often annotate the two or three dots that carry the argument and let the rest fade to context.

TEMPORAL VISUALIZATIONS

[/dfd_heading][ultimate_spacer height="20" height_on_tabs="20" height_on_tabs_portrait="20" height_on_mob_landscape="20" height_on_mob="20"][/vc_column][/vc_row][vc_row gap="20" bg_check="row-background-dark" css=".vc_custom_1696657851210{background-color: #2f2e3a !important;}"][vc_column][dfd_heading module_animation="transition.fadeIn" style="style_01" title_font_options="tag:h2|font_size:96|line_height:100|color:%23ffffff" subtitle_font_options="tag:h3" heading_margin="margin-top:20px;"]Connected Scatter Plot[/dfd_heading][vc_column_text css=""]

Best for: two measures moving through time

family: temporal

[/vc_column_text][ultimate_spacer height="20" height_on_tabs="20" height_on_tabs_portrait="20" height_on_mob_landscape="20" height_on_mob="20"][ultimate_spacer height="20" height_on_tabs="20" height_on_tabs_portrait="20" height_on_mob_landscape="20" height_on_mob="20"][/vc_column][vc_column width="1/6"][/vc_column][vc_column width="5/12"][vc_column_text]

A connected scatter plot traces how two variables travel together, year by year.

Join the dots of a scatter in date order and the chart becomes a path through time: petrol price against consumption, wages against productivity. Loops and reversals appear that neither line chart could show alone.

It rewards an audience willing to slow down, so we save it for reports and long-form pieces, and give it a guided annotation trail.

Best for: cyclical data with drama

family: temporal

[/vc_column_text][ultimate_spacer height="20" height_on_tabs="20" height_on_tabs_portrait="20" height_on_mob_landscape="20" height_on_mob="20"][ultimate_spacer height="20" height_on_tabs="20" height_on_tabs_portrait="20" height_on_mob_landscape="20" height_on_mob="20"][/vc_column][vc_column width="1/6"][/vc_column][vc_column width="1/4"][dfd_single_image image="37134" image_size="custom" image_width="300" image_height="300" hover_style="dfd-image-scale" image_border_radius="10"][ultimate_spacer height="20" height_on_tabs="20" height_on_tabs_portrait="20" height_on_mob_landscape="20" height_on_mob="20"][/vc_column][vc_column width="5/12"][vc_column_text]

The polar area diagram, Florence Nightingale’s rose, wraps time around a circle.

Each wedge is a period, and its area carries the value, which suits data with a built-in cycle: seasonal sales, monthly admissions, wind by compass point. Done well it is one of the most beautiful chart forms in the canon.

Areas are harder to compare than lengths, so we reserve it for stories where mood matters as much as precision.

The time series line chart is the workhorse of business reporting everywhere.

Revenue by month, users by week, temperature by decade; a line against a dated axis is the fastest route from spreadsheet to insight, and every stakeholder already knows how to read one. Trend, seasonality and shock all show at a glance.

Our rule: label the lines directly and retire the legend, because eyes should never commute between the two.

A cartogram redraws the map so each region’s size reflects its value, geography be damned.

A choropleth shades each region by its value, from pale to saturated.

It is the map everyone has seen: unemployment by state, sales by territory, rainfall by council area. The craft hides in the colour scale and in normalising the data, because raw totals just re-draw population.

Show rates per capita and the map starts saying something true.

Most charts fall into five working families: multidimensional charts like pies, histograms and scatter plots; temporal charts like time series; maps such as choropleths and cartograms; hierarchical charts like trees and sunbursts; and network diagrams like alluvial and node-link charts. Delivery formats sit on top: infographic reports, dashboards, presentations, interactives and animated videos.

[/vc_column_text][/vc_tta_section][vc_tta_section title="Which chart type is best for comparing categories?" tab_id="faqviztypes-2-2026"][vc_column_text css=""]

A bar chart wins nearly every time because human eyes judge aligned lengths better than angles or areas. Reach for a pie chart only when the point is a share of one whole with five or fewer slices.

[/vc_column_text][/vc_tta_section][vc_tta_section title="What is the difference between a dashboard and an infographic?" tab_id="faqviztypes-3-2026"][vc_column_text css=""]

A dashboard is a live tool that refreshes with the data and answers how are we tracking right now. An infographic report is a designed narrative, fixed at publication, that walks readers through an argument. Most organisations need both, doing different jobs.

[/vc_column_text][/vc_tta_section][vc_tta_section title="What tools do professionals use to build data visualizations?" tab_id="faqviztypes-4-2026"][vc_column_text css=""]

Power BI and Tableau dominate business dashboards, Excel and PowerPoint still carry everyday reporting, Adobe Illustrator handles publication-grade infographics, and After Effects drives animated data videos. The Datalabs Agency trains teams in the first four and produces in all of them.
