# Competitor register — Oddtoe

Who Oddtoe actually competes against for work: studios and makers who would
appear on the same shortlist when a museum, festival, council or ad agency picks
three names to invite.

**This is deliberately not Ahrefs' "organic competitors" list.** Ahrefs computes
competitors by keyword overlap, which surfaces directories and communities —
AWN, Stage32, Creativepool, The Rookies. None of them has ever bid against
Oddtoe and none ever will. Otto flagged this himself, and he is right: search
neighbours and commercial rivals are different sets, discovered different ways.

## The goal this serves

Otto's stated target: **be one of the three names on the shortlist** when a
museum or ad agency is choosing who to invite to pitch.

Worth being clear that getting shortlisted is not a search-ranking problem. A
producer building a shortlist draws on who they have used before, who peer
institutions used, who won something recently, and who a colleague recommends.
Search is at best a tie-breaker. So this register exists to answer *"who are we
up against, and what do they have that we don't"* — a positioning and evidence
question, not an SEO one.

## Status

**Empty.** No entries yet. Populate from real evidence, never from guesswork —
an invented rival is worse than an empty list.

## Fields

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| Studio | Trading name |
| Base | City — proximity matters for install work |
| Known for | The one thing a producer would name them for |
| Evidence | The specific project, award or roster that puts them in the set |
| Overlap | Which Oddtoe capability they compete on |
| Tier | See below |
| Source | Where the intel came from, and when |

## Tiers

- **Confirmed** — beat Oddtoe on a job, or bid the same job. Highest signal.
- **Likely** — verifiably does the same work for the same buyer type, no direct
  encounter yet.
- **Adjacent** — overlaps occasionally; a fabricator or animator who sometimes
  takes the whole job.

## How to find them, best signal first

1. **Who actually beat him.** The Business Development agent's lost-lead log
   captures "other quotes in play" and who won. This is the highest-signal
   source available and it currently holds **zero entries**. Every future loss
   logged is a name here. Nothing else comes close for accuracy.

2. **Credits on comparable delivered projects.** Who built the last several
   museum exhibitions, festival installations and brand activations in
   Melbourne? Credits are usually public — exhibition signage, festival
   programmes, agency case studies, LinkedIn posts. This is slow but it names
   the studios that actually win.

3. **Agency supplier rosters.** Taboo, Hardhat, Havas and Long Story Short all
   enquired via the form this year — meaning they were shopping. Whoever they
   used instead is a direct rival, and their case studies usually say who.

4. **Award shortlists.** Design and museum awards publish full shortlists, not
   just winners. A shortlist *is* a competitor set, already assembled.

5. **Public tender and EOI awards.** Councils and institutions publish awarded
   contracts. Slow, but definitive for public-art work — and the Baobab credit
   shows Oddtoe already delivers into a City of Melbourne venue.

6. **Industry directories.** Trade bodies and craft directories, not SEO ones.

## Where Ahrefs comes back in

Once a name is in this register, Ahrefs becomes genuinely useful on it: what that
studio ranks for, which of their pages pull traffic, what they publish. That is
competitor analysis worth doing — but only *after* the name arrived through
commercial evidence, never as the way the name was found.

Same for the content gap report: run it against **real rivals entered here**,
not against Ahrefs' auto-suggested overlap set. A content gap against AWN would
send Oddtoe chasing animation-industry news.

## Standing rule

No entry without a source and a date. If a studio is in here on a hunch, mark it
`Tier: Likely` and say the evidence is missing — do not let a guess harden into
a fact through repetition.
