# Competitors and influences — large-scale fabrication

Companies Oddtoe competes with **and** can learn from. Kept separate from
`experiential-agency-hitlist.md`, which is a target list: **nothing in this file is an outreach
prospect.**

Seeded 9 September 2026 out of the competitor analysis for the Protest & Press Day page
(`Oddtoe New Growth Pages 2026/protest-page-keyword-competitor-analysis.md`, section 9).

**Source quality — read before quoting any of this.** Everything below comes from the companies'
own marketing pages. Claims about capacity, client lists and process are **self-reported and
unverified**. Treat them as positioning, not audited fact. The PopHaus record was read directly off
their site on 9 Sep 2026 and is the more reliable of the two; the Scene2 record is still from search
snippets and should be re-read from source before it is relied on. No revenue or headcount figures
are recorded because none were published — do not fill those gaps with estimates.

## Scene2 — scene2.co.uk

*Record rewritten 9 Sep 2026 from `scene2.co.uk/giant-props-and-scenic-builds/` (their services
page) and `/giant-prop-fabrication-uk-2/` (a July 2026 blog article). The first version of this
file was built from search snippets and leant on the article, which is content marketing rather
than a statement of service.*

| | |
|---|---|
| Base | Essex, "just outside London", UK |
| Trading since | 1999 — 25+ years |
| Heritage | **Founding team came from theatrical design** |
| Named clients (self-reported) | Netflix, NBC Universal, Coca-Cola, John Lewis, Absolut, Warner Bros., LEGO, Carlsberg, Kat Von D Beauty, Debenhams |
| Pricing | **None published.** No price bands, no lead-time figures — "anywhere from a few weeks to a longer lead time" |

**Business model — asset-heavy, everything under one roof.** A 17,000 sq ft facility holding
design, fabrication, paint and finishing in the same building with the same team. The pitch is the
absence of handoffs: *"no third-party workshops, no coordination risk"*, *"one team accountable for
the whole thing, not a folder of phone numbers."*

**Six solution lines:** PR activations · immersive theatre and events · experiential activations
and pop-ups · custom exhibition stands · giant props and scenic builds · **shipping container and
vehicle conversions**.

**In-house capability, as they list it:** creative and technical design, CNC machining, carpentry,
metalwork, sculpture and prop making, scenic painting, vinyl and graphics, final finishing. Plus
structural engineering input, health and safety and **RAMS documentation**, transport, on-site
installation, takedown and **storage between outings**.

**Materials:** structural frame in timber or steel; sculpted, machined or fabricated surfaces;
CNC-routed foam cores; fibreglass, GRP and vacform for surface detail; scenic paint, vinyl and
specialist coatings. Also **zero-to-landfill sustainability**.

**Process:** creative and structural conversation → detailed engineering (drawings, load
calculations, fabrication plan) before any material is cut. Transport, access and site constraints
shape the design from session one; pieces are designed to break into sections when access demands.

**Named builds, with real construction detail:**

- **Kat Von D Beauty × Debenhams** — a five-metre studded lipstick. Plywood cylinder clad in
  CNC-carved polystyrene, sealed with a fire-retardant hard coat, hand-detailed studding, sprayed
  bright red and high-gloss black with a PVC chrome-effect finish. Shipped afterwards to Kat Von
  D's garden in Spain.
- **Carlsberg** — a giant chocolate bar with chocolate dart boards and edible glasses, as the
  centrepiece of an Easter PR stunt.
- **Warner Bros. × LEGO** — a full-scale LEGO-themed coffee shop in London.
- **Cardiff Airport, with Golley Slater** — a giant travel bag at baggage reclaim, closing a scenic
  arrivals journey through Welsh castle battlements, coastal walkways and mineshafts.

**Their sharpest argument, and it is aimed at us as much as at prop hire.** The whole giant-prop
article is a case against hire catalogues: a rented prop carries brand equity *for the prop house*,
and your idea gets fitted to what already exists. That part helps Oddtoe — it is an argument for
bespoke.

But the theatre heritage line cuts the other way: *"a set must perform night after night, not just
photograph well on press day"* and *"we design for the job, not just the look."* That is a direct
counter to photograph-first thinking — which is PopHaus's whole pitch **and the Protest & Press Day
page's central argument.** If a buyer has read Scene2, "designed backwards from the photograph"
will sound like a shortcut to them. Worth having an answer ready: the heads are built to be carried
for an hour and to come out again at the next action, which is a durability claim, not a photo one.

## PopHaus — pophaus.co.uk

*Record rewritten 9 Sep 2026 from the site itself. **Correction:** the first version of this file
said PopHaus outsources fabrication to "specialist workshops appointed per build" and called the
model asset-light. That came from a search snippet and is **wrong**. Their own site says fabrication
is handled **in-house at their own Brentford workshop**, explicitly contrasted against using
"whoever happens to have capacity". Both competitors are asset-heavy.*

| | |
|---|---|
| Base | Brentford, West London, UK |
| Contact | hello@pophaus.co.uk · 07964704027 |
| What they are | Bespoke oversized props, sculptural hero pieces and large-scale builds |
| Named clients (self-reported) | LEGO, Lipton, The Ivy Collection, Pride of Britain Awards, Captain Morgan |
| Sectors | Brand activations, PR stunts, product launches, retail spectacles, broadcast, experiential |

**Business model.** One accountable producer leads design, structural engineering, supplier
coordination, install, on-site running and de-rig; fabrication in their own workshop. The pitch to
buyers is commercial, not craft: *"One point of contact, one fixed-price quote, no margin stacked
across multiple agencies and contractors."*

**They publish their prices.** Unusual, and a competitive weapon:

| Tier | Size | Price | Lead time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small Giant | under **3 metres tall**, standard finish | £10k–£25k | 3–4 weeks |
| Mid-scale Hero | **3–6 metres tall**, complex finish, full structural engineering | £25k–£60k | 5–7 weeks |
| Flagship Hero | **6 metres tall and up**, premium finish, broadcast-grade | £60k–£150k+ | 7–10 weeks |

Rush builds add 15–25%. Range overall 1 week (simple replica) to 10–12 weeks (complex sculpt).

**Commercial terms:** no brief fee, fixed-price quote against signed-off scope, fully costed
response within one working day.

**Services:** eight named build categories — giant product replicas, sculptural hero builds, PR
stunt props, award-show and red-carpet scenic, retail window heroes, branded bars, archways and
entrance moments, pavilions and modular structures. Plus install, de-rig, storage and reuse.

**Materials and technology:** CNC, fibreglass, vac-form, hand-sculpt, foam carve, steel framing,
hybrid. Finishes include chrome, gloss, metallic, weathered, neon and glow-in-dark, brand-matched
and signed off before the full run.

**Process (6 steps):** brief and photo target → structural engineering and feasibility → workshop
and fabrication → paint, print and specialist finishing → transport, access and install → strike,
storage or sustainable disposal.

**Four things they do that Oddtoe does not:**

1. **"Designed backwards from the photograph."** Their organising question is *"what is the hero
   photograph this build has to deliver?"* — stress-tested at 3D-visualisation stage for angle,
   lighting position and scale relationship to people in the shot. This is **the same insight as
   the Protest & Press Day page**, arrived at independently. Validates the thinking, and means it
   is not a differentiator.
2. **A compliance pack as standard.** Structural calcs, fire-retardant material certificates, RAMS,
   CDM 2015 paperwork, and a venue/local-authority/insurer sign-off pack on request. *"The calc
   pack ships with the prop."* In the UK and EU this is table stakes, not a nicety.
3. **White-label as standard.** Unbranded crew blacks, unbranded site hoarding, NDA signed by
   default, *"credit stays with your agency."*
4. **Quantified reuse.** They store the piece for the next deployment and say storage cost is paid
   back inside the second redeployment. Unreused material is routed to timber and metal recycling,
   charity builds or community spaces.

## The three business models, side by side

| | Scene2 | PopHaus | Oddtoe |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capital | The building (17,000 sq ft) | Workshop + producer bench | The drawing hand |
| Fabrication | Fully in-house | Fully in-house (Brentford) | In-house, small scale |
| Where the value is claimed | No handoffs, no coordination risk | One accountable person, end to end | **Design authorship** |
| Materials | Steel, CNC foam, GRP, fibreglass, vacform | CNC, fibreglass, vac-form, foam carve, steel | Cane, papier-mâché |
| Geography | UK, van-distance | UK, van-distance | Melbourne → worldwide, crated |
| Who designs it | The client briefs, they build | The client briefs, they build | Oddtoe draws it first |
| Pricing | **Not published — no figures at all** | **Published: £10k–£150k+ in three tiers** | Not published |
| Credit | Not stated | **White-label — credit stays with the agency** | Authored, by name |

**The one that matters:** neither of them designs the thing. Both take a brief, mood board or
finished concept and realise it. Oddtoe's claim is authorship — a political cartoonist's judgement
about what the picture has to say — and that is genuinely not on either of their menus.

**The one that cuts against us:** their material systems are industrial and heavy. Steel and GRP
are durable, engineerable and expensive to ship. Cane and paper are light, crate flat and cross
borders — an advantage for international work and a liability if a client wants something to stand
outdoors for a season. Know which conversation you are in.

## What to actually borrow

1. **Engineering language.** Both lead with structural engineering — *drawings, load calculations,
   a fabrication plan before anything is cut*. That is what de-risks a five-figure build for a
   procurement-minded buyer, and Oddtoe's pages do not currently say it anywhere. It is true of the
   work already, so this is a wording gap, not a capability gap.
2. **Naming the finish standard.** Scene2 sells "high-durability paints and scenic finishes applied
   by the same team that built the piece." Specificity about finish is a trust signal.
3. **The de-rig.** PopHaus sells install, on-site running **and de-rig** as named line items. Otto's
   package covers the day but not the pack-down as an explicit deliverable.
4. **Publish price bands.** PopHaus gives three tiers with sizes, lead times and numbers on a public
   page. It disqualifies tyre-kickers before they email and it anchors the conversation. Oddtoe's
   pages say "does not publish prices", which is a defensible position but a slower one.
5. **Quantify the reuse claim.** PopHaus says storage cost is paid back inside the second
   redeployment. The Protest page argues reuse but never puts a number on it.
6. **Answer inside one working day, no brief fee.** Both a service promise and a filter. Cheap to
   offer, and it is the kind of thing a procurement-minded buyer notices.
7. **Carry a compliance pack.** Structural calcs, fire-retardant certificates, RAMS and CDM 2015
   paperwork ship with the prop for venue and insurer sign-off. For anything landing at a UK or EU
   venue this will be asked for, and not having it is a lost job rather than a smaller one.

## What not to borrow

- **Do not compete on capacity.** 17,000 sq ft is not a claim to match or imply.
- **Do not adopt "we build what you brief."** It is their model and it discards the thing Oddtoe
  has that they do not.
- **Do not copy the client-logo wall** until there are logos that can be named with permission.
- **Do not go white-label.** PopHaus wins PR-agency work partly by disappearing — unbranded crew,
  NDA by default, *"credit stays with your agency."* That is the correct move for a fabricator and
  the wrong one for Oddtoe, whose whole claim is authorship. It is worth knowing the tension exists
  before walking into a PR agency: they may well want an invisible supplier, and that is a
  different business. See `oddtoe-artist-not-only-for-hire` in memory.

| 9 Sep 2026 | PopHaus | Tier 2 "Mid-scale Hero" — 3–6 metres tall, complex finish, full structural engineering | £25k–£60k | 5–7 weeks | as above |
| 9 Sep 2026 | PopHaus | Tier 3 "Flagship Hero" — 6 metres tall and up, premium finish, broadcast-grade | £60k–£150k+ | 7–10 weeks | as above |
| 9 Sep 2026 | PopHaus | Rush premium | +15–25% on base quote | from 1 week | as above |
| 9 Sep 2026 | Scene2 | Giant props and scenic builds | **None published** | **None published** — "a few weeks to a longer lead time" | scene2.co.uk services + article |

**Tiers are banded by height in metres** — under 3m tall, 3–6m, 6m and up. Not months, not
millions.

**Who is being paid, and for what.** The figure is what the client pays PopHaus in total, as one
fixed-price quote. Their words: each band *"assumes full supply: concept, structural engineering,
supplier appointment, fabrication, paint, install and de-rig"*, and the model is *"one point of
contact, one fixed-price quote, **no margin stacked across multiple agencies and contractors**."*

That last clause is the actual sales argument. The normal chain is brand → creative or PR agency →
production company → fabricator, with a markup added at each step. PopHaus positions as collapsing
that to a single margin. So £10k–£25k is not a workshop's build cost — it is a build **plus**
engineering, finishing, transport, a crew on site and the pack-down afterwards, with one company's
profit in it.

**What their page does not break out:** the split between design, fabrication, labour and install
is not published, and no day rates are given. Do not infer one. Storage for reuse appears to be
charged separately — they say storage cost is repaid inside the second redeployment — and the rush
premium of 15–25% sits on top of the band.

**A contradiction in their own copy, worth knowing.** The pricing section says "supplier
appointment", implying outsourced fabrication, while the rest of the page insists fabrication is
in-house at Brentford. This is the same ambiguity that produced the error corrected above. The
in-house claim is made more often and more recently, so it is the one recorded — but if this ever
matters commercially, ask them rather than trusting either line.

**Reading it.** PopHaus publishes; Scene2 gives no figure anywhere. Two defensible strategies —
transparency filters enquiries and anchors the number, silence protects margin and keeps every job
a conversation. Oddtoe currently sits with Scene2 ("does not publish prices"), which is a choice
worth making deliberately rather than by default.

**Currency note:** all figures GBP as published. No conversion is stored — rates move, and a
converted number in this file would go stale and be quoted as fact.
