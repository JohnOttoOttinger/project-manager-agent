## Short bio (~90 words — podcast intros, press boilerplate, form fields)

Oddtoe is a Melbourne artist and animator working across generative AI
animation, experiential design and public art. He has drawn under the Oddtoe
name since 1996; his professional practice runs from 2006 and includes work
for organisations such as National Geographic. Earlier practice spans
political cartooning, puppetry, data visualisation and street art. He runs a
hybrid animation pipeline: generative AI for imagery and movement, rigging for
consistency, traditional motion design for type, graphics and edit. His
delivered public work includes *Babbling with Baobabs* (Signal, City of
Melbourne — Melbourne International Comedy Festival, 2019).

## Long bio (~210 words — media kit, about page, speaker submissions)

Oddtoe is a Melbourne artist and animator working across generative AI
animation, experiential design and public art — an artist-led practice that
both designs and fabricates. He has worked under the Oddtoe name since 1996,
with professional practice from 2006 that includes work for organisations
such as National Geographic. His background runs through political
cartooning, gag cartoons, puppetry, data visualisation and street art;
illustration remains the studio's foundational skill.

The studio's animation work runs on a hybrid pipeline: generative AI
produces imagery and movement, rigging keeps characters consistent, and
traditional motion design carries type, graphics and edit. Art direction,
project management and quality control stay in one pair of hands. This puts
Oddtoe in an unusual position in the argument about AI in animation: a
working animator with twenty years of traditional craft who uses these tools
in production every day.

Beyond the screen, the practice covers kinetic and public sculpture,
installation, projection, prop design and fabrication, topiary and sensory
garden design, and robotics. *Babbling with Baobabs*, commissioned for
Signal by the City of Melbourne for the 2019 Melbourne International Comedy
Festival, is the studio's delivered public work. Oddtoe also publishes an
annually updated guide to the world's animation conferences, which ranks in
Google's top three for its category.

## Artist statement — APPROVED (Otto, 18 Aug 2026)

Option A with the closing line adapted from Option C ("their audience" had no
referent in A, so it reads "audiences" — flag if that adaptation is wrong).

Every job I've had comes down to the same thing: getting people to look. I
started out drawing — political cartoons, gag panels — then spent years in
data visualization, including for National Geographic, turning spreadsheets
into pictures people actually wanted to read. Along the way I figured out
what I was really chasing: that small jolt when a person sees something and
says, oh — look at that.

Visualization, art and awe. That's the whole shop. Some days it comes out as
animation: my studio runs a hybrid pipeline where generative AI produces the
imagery and movement, rigging keeps the characters consistent, and twenty
years of drawing tells me when a frame is wrong. Other days it's physical —
puppets, props, projections, sculpture, topiary and sensory gardens. One of
them was *Babbling with Baobabs*, a show for Signal and the City of
Melbourne at the Melbourne International Comedy Festival.

I think our public spaces should be quirkier and more memorable than they
are — I've stood up in council meetings and said so. The best result I know
is a stranger stopping in front of a piece of mine, laughing, and waving
someone else over to look. I want audiences walking away grinning.
